## Suggested publishing notes

- **Best time:** 8:00 GMT (Sunday morning — booked parents are often doing their mental Sunday checklist for the week ahead; this lands at the moment they're thinking "wait, is tomorrow actually set?"). A warm "yes, it's set, see you tomorrow" post arriving right then is proof-of-care in its purest form.
- **If posting both:** Space them ~8 hours apart so the teen post lands ~4:00 PM GMT (Sunday afternoon — the "ugh, school tomorrow" window where "actually something different and good is happening tomorrow" lands well emotionally).
- **Tone discipline:** This is NOT a sales post. If your instinct halfway through writing is to add "limited seats" or "last chance" — resist. The weekend-prep + night-before pair exists to *earn trust*, not to close waverers. The last line of Post 1 does include a soft open-door for last-minute bookings (we'd be silly not to) — but the whole post should read first as a message to families who already booked. The undecided parent still watching sees how the booked families are being treated and updates their estimate of what their own child's week will feel like. That's the conversion mechanism this weekend. Proof-of-care, not a pitch.
- **Engagement tip:** The "reply if anything goes sideways in the first ten minutes" line is deliberate. It (a) reassures nervous parents that there's a human on the other end tomorrow morning, (b) makes the channel feel staffed and responsive — not a broadcast firehose — and (c) means that if anyone *does* reply, the thread tomorrow morning will be full of real human help, which is itself social proof for undecided parents reading next week. If possible, be ready to actually reply to anything that comes in tomorrow between 8:00–10:00 GMT.
- **Pin update:** This is the moment to flip the pin. Monday's "10 weeks + half-term next week" pin has done its job. Replace with: *"🚀 Half-term week is here — session one Monday 8:00 GMT. Booked families: see you tomorrow. Still deciding: door's open, link's at codeillustrated.com/academy."* The pin now reads as *in-progress*, which is what you want the channel to feel like all week.
- **Honesty note:** Nothing in this post needs real-data edits — it's intentionally non-specific because the warmth matters more than the specifics tonight. The only edit worth making is one or two sentences of **your own voice** at the top — e.g. a one-liner on how you spent the weekend prepping ("The slides are done, the project templates are loaded, I've triple-checked the links"). That human-behind-the-channel line will outperform anything written from a template, because tomorrow morning parents want to trust the person running the room, not a brand.
- **Continuity note:** Mon 19 landed the ask. Tue 20 handled logistics. Wed 21 showed what kids build. Thu 22 added parent voice. Fri 23 was last-call. Sat 24 was weekend prep for booked families. Today closes the pre-week arc with the night-before beat. **Tomorrow (Mon 26) = session-one-day** — the single highest-leverage post of the whole month. Suggested shape: short, from inside the session — even one phrase like "Session one just started. 23 kids online, 3 projects already named. Here we go. 🚀" (adjust numbers to real). If you can post a *photo of a screen* with student permission (code they wrote in session one, a half-built game, a hand-raised question in the chat) attached to Tuesday or Wednesday's post, that single image will be the most-shared post of the week — parents forward real evidence more than any amount of polished copy. The arc for the rest of half-term week is **inside-the-room updates, not outside-the-room pitches.** Think "a running programme has a channel now" rather than "a channel is still trying to sell a programme." If any seats are still filling late (unlikely but possible for the 8-11 track which often books slowly), one short "two beginner seats left — if your child is nervous about joining mid-week, we've slotted new starters before" post around Wed morning would still convert. Otherwise: pure proof-of-delivery all week.
