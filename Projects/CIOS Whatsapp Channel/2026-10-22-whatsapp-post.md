Parents — Thursday. Parent-to-parent post. 💬

Three days of posts this week have been *us* talking to you. Today we hand the mic over.

Here's the sentence we hear most from parents the week after a holiday programme — paraphrased from the hundreds we've taught, not one person's quote:

> *"The surprise wasn't that she learned to code. The surprise was that she sat down on her own on Saturday morning to carry on with it."* 🪑

That's the moment that matters.

Not the certificate. Not the finished game. **The Saturday-morning moment** when the laptop opens without you asking. When "I'm bored" gets replaced by "I'm figuring something out." When your child discovers that building is *more interesting* than scrolling. 📵 → 🛠️

Here's what parents tell us, honestly, afterwards:

🧩 *"He's less on his phone and I didn't have to fight him about it."*
🗣️ *"She explained what a 'variable' was to her grandfather. He had no idea what she was talking about but he was proud."*
📚 *"Her teacher noticed. She started asking better questions in maths too."*
🤷 *"I thought it would be babysitting with a laptop. It wasn't."*

None of these are "my child became a prodigy." They're smaller than that. And more real.

The honest version: not every child comes out of a half-term programme obsessed with coding. Some do. Most come out *curious* — and curiosity, in a 10-year-old, is the whole game. 🎯
