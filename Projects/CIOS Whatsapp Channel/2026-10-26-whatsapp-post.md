## Suggested publishing notes

- **Best time for Post 1:** 8:00 GMT exactly — ideally within 15 minutes of session one actually starting, so the "live now" framing is literally true. If session one starts later than 8:00 GMT in your actual schedule, align the post to the real start time, not the fired-at time of this document.
- **Replace the [X] placeholders with real numbers before posting.** This is the single most important edit of the day. "Laptops open across 4 countries" or "17 kids on the beginners' track" lands orders of magnitude harder than the bracketed version — specificity is the whole move. Even imperfect real numbers ("roughly 20 kids online this morning") beat polished vague copy. Three numbers to pull from your dashboard before 8:00 GMT: (1) total kids live, (2) countries represented, (3) track breakdown. If any one of those is embarrassing (e.g. only 3 kids on intermediate), either drop that line entirely or merge the counts ("20+ kids across all three tracks"). Never invent a number.
- **If posting both Post 1 and Post 2:** This is the recommended shape for today. 8:00 GMT (live-now) + 5:00 PM GMT (day-one-done) frames the day as an arc — the channel reads as a programme people are *inside,* not watching. The late-afternoon post hits parents during school pick-up / commute scroll — a strong window.
- **If posting Post 1 + Post 3 instead:** Only if you don't have the bandwidth for an end-of-day post. Three posts is too many for a Monday.
- **Pin update:** Flip the pin from Sunday's "see you tomorrow" line to something that reads as *now:* *"🚀 Half-term week is live. Session one running Mondays–Fridays, 8:00 GMT. Door open on the 8–11 beginner track for one more late-start today. codeillustrated.com/academy"* Keep it short — the pin is a billboard, not an essay.
- **Photo/screenshot opportunity (highest-leverage of the week):** If you can post **one real screenshot** attached to Post 2 today, or to Tuesday morning's post — a kid's code, a half-built game, the chat window with 20 names, a screen with "Hello World!" showing — that single image will outperform every other post this half-term. Get permission from one family before 10 AM. One screenshot of a real child's real project is worth more than ten weeks of polished copy. If getting permission in time is tricky, a screenshot of **your own screen** showing the instructor's view (code editor + chat window + a muted student grid) is almost as good and needs no permission.
- **Engagement tip:** The "drop a 🚀 if your child is in session one right now" line is designed to spike early engagement — booked parents watching from a coffee shop or on their commute will tap it instinctively, which pushes the post up the channel's visibility algorithm for undecided parents arriving later. Even 4–5 🚀 reactions in the first hour meaningfully changes who sees the post next. This is the one day of the half-term where reaction-prompts earn their place; the rest of the week should lean on substance over prompts.
- **Reply-watch for the morning:** Yesterday's post set the expectation that someone's at the other end if anything goes sideways in the first ten minutes of session one. Honour that today — be ready to reply to any inbound messages between 8:00–10:00 GMT. Any public reply thread you build this morning (even "hey, your laptop screen is sharing fine — try the mute button in the top-right") becomes social proof for every undecided parent reading the channel this week. The thread is the delivery.
- **Honesty note:** The post leans on "live now" energy — only publish it if session one is actually running. If something unexpected delays session one (weather, instructor illness, platform issue), **do not post Post 1 as-is.** A "live now" post published while the session isn't live is the single fastest way to burn trust the week has built. If delayed, pivot to a short "we're pushing session one back to [new time] — details to parents by DM / email in the next 10 minutes" post. The channel is far more forgiving of real delays named clearly than of posts that don't match the reality everyone can see.
- **Continuity note for Tuesday 27 Oct onwards:** The arc from here is *inside-the-room, every day.* Suggested shape for the week: **Tue 27** — "Day 2: here's what [specific kid, by first name only + permission] built this morning" with a 1-line description + screenshot if possible. **Wed 28** — mid-week progress post, maybe a short clip of a kid demoing (if permission). **Thu 29** — a quiet "we're on the home stretch" post + a teaser of what Friday's demo-day will look like. **Fri 30** — the **demo day post**, by far the second-highest-leverage of the half-term, built around 2–3 real projects named by first name with screenshots. The whole week's purpose is to end Friday with a channel full of real evidence of real builds. That becomes the November recruiting asset: every post this week is a brick in the wall that convinces the next cohort of parents in a few weeks' time. **Nov recruiting cycle:** Starting Mon 2 Nov, the arc shifts from "half-term is live" to "look what half-term kids built — next intake [date]." The 10-week programme that was teased in the Mon 19 Oct post needs its own ask-cycle in early November, building on this week's evidence.
