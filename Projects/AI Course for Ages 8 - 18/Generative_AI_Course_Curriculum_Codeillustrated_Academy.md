## 1. Course Philosophy & Framework

Generative AI — tools that create new text, images, audio, video, or code rather than just analyzing existing data — is now the dominant way young people encounter AI, and it carries a different risk and skill profile than "classic" AI (recommendation engines, classifiers). This course replaces the academy's general AI curriculum with a track built specifically around generative AI: how it works, how to direct it well, and how to use it responsibly.

Three tiers, each running two dense 60-minute live sessions per week:

- **GenAI Sparks (8–10)** — creative, guided, entirely supervised-tool play. No independent accounts.
- **GenAI Creators (11–14)** — real prompt engineering, real (kid-safe) tools, first serious ethics/legal content.
- **GenAI Innovators (15–18)** — technical depth (how transformers and diffusion models work), building an actual generative AI application, and full engagement with law, policy, and career pathways.

Design principles specific to generative AI:

- **"AI literacy, not AI advocacy."** Following Common Sense Media's framing, the goal is for students to understand what generative AI can and can't do and how to avoid its harms — not simply to maximize their use of it.
- **Every tool decision is a safety decision.** Which product a student touches matters as much as what they're taught. Tool selection below is deliberately age-gated (see Section 3).
- **Two sessions/week = instruction + studio.** Session A each week introduces a concept; Session B is hands-on studio/lab time applying it — dense, not padded.
- **Ethics and law are load-bearing, not a footnote.** Deepfakes, misinformation, and copyright now carry real legal consequences for minors (see Section 7) — this course treats that as core curriculum, especially from Tier 2 onward.

## 3. Tool & Safety Baseline (read before scheduling any tier)

Generative AI tools are not interchangeable, and several popular ones are not legally usable by minors without specific safeguards. This baseline governs every tier below.

| Tool category | Recommended for this course | Age gating notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sketch-to-image (no true generation) | AutoDraw | Safe for all ages — converts sketches to icons, no open-ended image generation |
| Guided image generation | Canva AI (school/instructor-managed account) | Strong safety record for ages 9+; still instructor-supervised for under-13s |
| Guided image generation (older) | Adobe Firefly | Trained on licensed/stock content only; recommended 13+ |
| Guided generation under 13 | Microsoft Copilot with Family Safety controls, or instructor-managed account only | Never an individual under-13 personal account |
| Purpose-built school AI chat | Khanmigo, Askie for Schools, or equivalent COPPA-compliant K–8 product | Safety is built into the product, not a filter bolted onto an adult tool |
| Consumer chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, etc.) | Tier 3 only, via a supervised school-managed account | Under-13 use of consumer chatbots violates most providers' terms of service and is not COPPA-compliant by default |
| LLM APIs for building apps | Tier 3 only | School-managed API key, logged usage, signed responsible-use agreement |

**Non-negotiable rule:** no student under 13 is given an individual account on a consumer generative AI product. All under-13 tool use happens on instructor-managed accounts or purpose-built, COPPA-compliant school tools. Before deploying any AI tool to under-13 students, the academy must have a signed Data Processing Agreement (DPA) with the vendor and explicit COPPA-compliant language in its terms.

## 4. Tier 1 — GenAI Sparks (Ages 8–10)

### Overview

Focus: understanding that some AI *makes new things* (pictures, stories, sounds) rather than just sorting or recognizing; that generative AI "guesses" based on patterns and can be wrong or make things up; and building an early instinct to ask "could this be AI-made?" No independent accounts — all tool use is instructor-led and projected, or done on shared/managed devices.

**Tools:** AutoDraw (sketch-to-icon), a purpose-built K–8 school AI chat tool (e.g., Khanmigo-style, instructor account), Microsoft Copilot with Family Safety (instructor-managed, for guided image generation demos), printed card/sorting activities.

**Learning outcomes:**
- Can explain the difference between an AI that "recognizes" something and one that "makes up" something new.
- Understands that generative AI can be wrong, make things up, or produce a "too weird to be real" result.
- Can name at least one reason to ask a trusted adult before using or trusting anything an AI creates.

### Weekly Session Breakdown

| Wk | Session A (Concept) | Session B (Studio) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is Generative AI? AI that "makes" vs. AI that "sorts" | Studio: "Made by a person or by AI?" sorting game with printed examples |
| 2 | How AI Draws — sketches, patterns, guesses | Studio: AutoDraw sketch parade — turn doodles into icons, compare guesses |
| 3 | AI That Tells Stories (instructor-led demo on school AI chat tool) | Studio: Build-a-story — students give ideas, instructor drives the tool, class edits the result together |
| 4 | Magic Words — intro to prompts (what you ask changes what you get) | Studio: Prompt play on a guided image tool (instructor account); compare two prompts side by side |
| 5 | When AI Gets It Wrong — funny/silly AI mistakes | Studio: "Fix the AI's Mistake" — spot what's wrong in a set of AI-made examples |
| 6 | Is It Real or AI-Made? — first media-literacy pass | Studio: Real-vs-AI picture sorting game with a class discussion of clues |
| 7 | Being Safe & Kind with AI — privacy, asking a grown-up, no personal info | Studio: Role-play scenarios ("What would you do if...") |
| 8 | AI Helpers at Home — voice assistants and generated answers | Studio: "Interview a Smart Speaker" guided activity |
| 9 | My First AI-Assisted Creation — project kickoff | Studio: Build time — mini project combining a drawing + a short AI-assisted story |
| 10 | Showcase prep — practicing what to say about your project | Studio: Showcase — share creations with family; certificates |

### Sample Full Lesson Plan — Week 4, Session A + B: "Magic Words"

**Objective:** Students will observe that changing the words in a prompt changes the AI's output, and describe one way to make a prompt "more specific."

**Materials:** Projector, instructor-managed guided image tool account, printed "prompt cards" with simple describing words (color, size, feeling, place).

**Session A — Concept (60 min):**
- Warm-up (10 min): "If you asked a friend to draw 'an animal,' what might they draw? What if you said 'a fluffy orange cat sitting in the sun'?" Discuss how more detail helps.
- Direct instruction (20 min): Instructor runs the same short prompt twice on the guided tool ("a dog"), then a detailed version ("a small fluffy brown dog playing with a red ball in a park"), comparing results live. Introduce the word "prompt" as "the words we use to ask AI for something."
- Guided group activity (25 min): As a class, students take turns suggesting one added detail to a prompt; instructor runs it after each addition so students see the image evolve.
- Wrap-up (5 min): "What's one word you could add to a prompt to make it better?"

**Session B — Studio (60 min):**
- Warm-up (5 min): Recap "magic words" from Session A.
- Hands-on activity (40 min): In small groups (instructor drives the account, students dictate), each group writes a simple prompt on a prompt card, adds 2–3 descriptive details, and runs it. Groups compare their "before" and "after" prompts and images.
- Reflection (10 min): Gallery walk of printed/projected results — "Which detail made the biggest difference?"
- Wrap-up (5 min): Preview Week 5 — "Next time, we'll see what happens when AI gets it wrong."

**Differentiation:** Provide a picture-based prompt-card option for pre-readers; extension groups try a 4th descriptive detail and predict the result before running it.

**Checks for understanding:** Each group verbally states one detail they added and what changed in the image.

### Assessment Approach

No formal grades. Sticker/digital badge per completed studio session. "GenAI Spark" certificate at the Week 10 showcase.

## 5. Tier 2 — GenAI Creators (Ages 11–14)

### Overview

Focus: real prompt engineering skills, hands-on use of kid-safe generative tools (Canva AI for images, a school-managed text tool), a conceptual (non-technical) understanding of how text and image generators work, and the first serious pass at deepfakes, misinformation, and the legal risk of misusing generative tools — content Common Sense Media specifically recommends starting at this age band (its AI Literacy Lessons target grades 6–12).

**Tools:** Canva AI (school-managed accounts), a school-managed text-generation tool with logging, printed case studies, class debate format for ethics weeks.

**Learning outcomes:**
- Can independently write and iteratively refine a prompt to get a specific result (format, tone, style).
- Can explain, at a conceptual level, that text generators predict likely next words and image generators build images from patterns in training data — and that neither "knows" facts the way a person does.
- Can identify a deepfake or AI-generated misinformation example and explain why it's misleading.
- Understands that creating a realistic fake image or video of a real person — including another minor — can carry serious legal consequences, not just school consequences.
- Can describe a basic copyright/attribution consideration when using AI-generated content.

### Weekly Session Breakdown

| Wk | Session A (Concept) | Session B (Studio) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What Makes AI "Generative"? Text, image, audio, video generation overview | Studio: Tool tour — explore examples across formats |
| 2 | How Text Generators Work — predicting the next word, training data | Studio: "Be the AI" — human word-prediction game |
| 3 | Prompt Engineering Basics — being specific, giving context | Studio: Prompt lab on school text tool — iterate for a better result |
| 4 | Prompt Engineering, Advanced — role, format, examples (few-shot) | Studio: Prompt challenge — best result for a set brief, timed |
| 5 | AI Image Generation & Style | Studio: Canva AI project — create an image set in a chosen style |
| 6 | How Image Generators Work — patterns, diffusion (simplified, visual) | Studio: Compare how small prompt changes shift the image |
| 7 | When AI Gets It Wrong — hallucinations & fact-checking | Studio: Fact-check a set of AI-generated "facts" against real sources |
| 8 | Bias in Generated Content | Studio: Test the same prompt with small demographic changes; discuss patterns |
| 9 | Deepfakes & Misinformation — what they are, how they spread | Studio: Spot-the-deepfake activity with real (age-appropriate) examples |
| 10 | The Law & Deepfakes — why creating fake images/videos of real people is a serious legal issue, not just a prank | Studio: Case-study discussion + "what would you do" scenarios |
| 11 | Who Owns AI-Made Art? Copyright & attribution basics | Studio: Capstone kickoff — plan a multimedia project with proper sourcing habits |
| 12 | Capstone build & rehearsal | Studio: Capstone showcase & peer review; certificates |

### Sample Full Lesson Plan — Week 10, Session A + B: "The Law & Deepfakes"

**Objective:** Students will explain why creating a realistic fake image or video of a real person can have legal consequences, and identify at least one responsible-use rule they will follow.

**Materials:** Two short, age-appropriate case summaries (a school-deepfake incident and its real consequences, written in plain language without graphic detail), discussion prompts, whiteboard.

**Session A — Concept (60 min):**
- Warm-up (10 min): Recap Week 9's "spot the deepfake" — "Now that you can often spot one, what happens once it's already been shared?"
- Direct instruction (25 min): Instructor explains, in plain terms: deepfake tools have made it easy to create realistic fake images/videos of real people; several jurisdictions now treat creating nonconsensual sexual or realistic-harassing deepfakes of another minor as seriously as other forms of image-based abuse, with real legal consequences for the minor who created it — this is not just a school discipline issue. Emphasize this is about protecting classmates and taking the tool seriously, not fear-mongering.
- Case discussion (20 min): Walk through one simplified real-world case; discuss what happened, why it mattered, and what the better choice would have been.
- Wrap-up (5 min): Introduce the academy's responsible-use agreement students will sign.

**Session B — Studio (60 min):**
- Warm-up (5 min): Quick poll — "Before today, did you know this could be illegal, not just against the rules?"
- Small-group activity (35 min): Groups work through 3 "what would you do" scenarios (e.g., a friend shares a fake image of a classmate; someone asks you to help make one "as a joke"; you see one being shared in a group chat) and present their response.
- Class discussion (15 min): Compare group responses; instructor reinforces: don't create, don't share, tell a trusted adult.
- Wrap-up (5 min): Preview Week 11 — from protecting others' images to respecting others' creative work (copyright).

**Differentiation:** Provide a simplified one-paragraph case summary for students who need it; extension groups research how a chosen state or country currently handles this legally.

**Checks for understanding:** Each student writes one sentence: "One thing I'll do if I see this happening."

### Capstone Options (choose one)

- An illustrated short story combining AI-generated text and images, with a sourcing/attribution page.
- A "spot the fake" awareness poster or short presentation for younger students.
- A style-consistent image set (e.g., a comic strip or album cover concept) built through iterative prompting.

### Assessment Approach

Rubric across four criteria: prompt-craft skill, creativity, conceptual understanding, and ethics/legal reasoning (each 1–4). Certificates awarded at completion; responsible-use agreement signed by student and parent before Week 5's image-generation module.

## 6. Tier 3 — GenAI Innovators (Ages 15–18)

### Overview

Focus: technical depth (how LLMs and diffusion models actually work), building a real generative AI application under supervision, and full engagement with the legal, policy, and career landscape of generative AI — positioning strong capstones for college applications or competitions.

**Tools:** Python via Jupyter/Colab, a supervised school-managed LLM API account (logged usage, signed responsible-use agreement), Adobe Firefly or Canva AI for image work, tokenizer visualization tools, a guided retrieval-augmented-generation (RAG) sandbox.

**Learning outcomes:**
- Can explain tokens, embeddings, and next-token prediction in an LLM, and attention at a conceptual level.
- Can explain how diffusion models generate images, at a conceptual level.
- Can write advanced prompts (few-shot, chain-of-thought, system prompts) and chain multiple prompts into a workflow.
- Can build and run a small generative AI application using an API, and red-team it for hallucination, bias, and unsafe output.
- Can discuss copyright/IP questions around AI-generated content and the current legal treatment of deepfakes, including criminal liability risk for minors.
- Can discuss current AI policy/regulation and identify possible career or study pathways in the field.

### Weekly Session Breakdown

| Wk | Session A (Concept) | Session B (Studio) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Landscape of Generative AI — text, image, audio, video, code; brief history | Studio: Hands-on tool tour across modalities |
| 2 | How LLMs Work I — tokens, embeddings, next-token prediction | Studio: Tokenizer exploration exercise |
| 3 | How LLMs Work II — transformer architecture & attention (conceptual) | Studio: Interactive attention-visualization demo |
| 4 | Advanced Prompt Engineering — few-shot, chain-of-thought, system prompts | Studio: Prompt lab, structured challenges |
| 5 | Prompt Chaining — building multi-step workflows | Studio: Build a simple chained-prompt tool |
| 6 | How Image & Video Generators Work — diffusion models, brief GAN history | Studio: Image-generation project (style transfer / iterative refinement) |
| 7 | Fine-Tuning, RAG & Customization Concepts | Studio: Guided RAG demo in a school-provided sandbox |
| 8 | Building a GenAI App I — environment & API setup | Studio: First supervised API call, basic script |
| 9 | Building a GenAI App II — adding logic, input handling, simple UI | Studio: Build session with instructor support |
| 10 | Evaluating AI Outputs — hallucination, bias, and toxicity testing | Studio: Red-team your own app; log and categorize failures |
| 11 | Copyright, IP & the Law of AI-Generated Content | Studio: Case-study analysis (training data lawsuits, ownership of AI output) |
| 12 | Deepfakes, Digital Forensics & Legislation — current laws, criminal liability for minors, detection techniques | Studio: Hands-on deepfake-detection tool practice |
| 13 | AI Policy, Regulation & Careers — EU AI Act, US state laws, industry roles | Studio: Structured debate + career-pathway panel prep |
| 14 | Capstone build & rehearsal | Studio: Capstone presentations & course wrap-up; certificates |

### Sample Full Lesson Plan — Week 12, Session A + B: "Deepfakes, Digital Forensics & Legislation"

**Objective:** Students will describe the current legal landscape around deepfakes (including liability risk for minors), apply at least one detection technique to a media sample, and articulate the academy's responsible-use policy in their own words.

**Materials:** Laptops, a deepfake-detection tool or guided walkthrough, printed summaries of relevant current laws (kept general and updated per jurisdiction), sample media set (mix of real and clearly-labeled synthetic content for practice — no real victims' content used).

**Session A — Concept (60 min):**
- Warm-up (10 min): "What's changed technically since deepfakes first appeared, and why is detection getting harder?"
- Direct instruction (30 min): Cover how deepfakes are generated (diffusion/GAN recap), current detection approaches (artifact analysis, metadata, provenance/watermarking standards), and the legal landscape: several jurisdictions now criminalize creating or distributing nonconsensual realistic sexual or harassing deepfakes, and minors are not automatically exempt from liability. Emphasize this is a fast-changing legal area and the academy's policy is deliberately conservative: never create a realistic image/video/audio of a real person without clear, informed consent.
- Discussion (15 min): Why does provenance/watermarking help, and why isn't it a complete solution?
- Wrap-up (5 min): Preview studio detection practice.

**Session B — Studio (60 min):**
- Warm-up (5 min): Predict — "Which of these clues do you think will help you the most in spotting a fake?"
- Hands-on lab (40 min): Students use a detection tool/checklist on a prepared sample set, record their confidence and reasoning, then compare against the answer key and discuss what they missed.
- Reflection (10 min): "Where did your gut instinct fail you? What made this one tricky?"
- Wrap-up (5 min): Restate the responsible-use rule and preview Week 13's policy/careers session.

**Differentiation:** Provide a structured detection checklist for students who want more scaffolding; extension students research a specific jurisdiction's current deepfake law and present a one-minute summary.

**Checks for understanding:** Each student submits their detection reasoning for one sample as an exit ticket; instructor reviews for correct application of at least two detection criteria.

### Capstone Project

Individually or in pairs, students design and build a small generative AI application (a text tool, an image tool, or a chained-prompt workflow) with documented safety testing. Deliverables: working code/notebook, a one-page write-up covering technical approach and ethical considerations, and a presentation. Strong capstones are packaged into a portfolio for college applications, competitions, or internship use.

### Assessment Approach

Rubric across four dimensions: technical depth, originality, ethical/legal reasoning, and presentation quality (each 1–5). Written reflection required. Portfolio packet issued on request.

## 7. Cross-Cutting Ethics, Safety & Legal Framework

| Theme | Tier 1 (8–10) | Tier 2 (11–14) | Tier 3 (15–18) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hallucination / "AI can be wrong" | "AI can make things up" via silly examples | Fact-checking practice against real sources | Systematic red-teaming of their own app's outputs |
| Bias in generated content | Not covered directly | Test prompts for stereotype patterns | Bias/toxicity evaluation as part of app testing |
| Deepfakes & misinformation | Early "real or AI-made?" media literacy | Spot-the-deepfake + why it's a legal issue, not a prank | Detection techniques + current legislation, criminal liability for minors |
| Copyright / IP | Not covered directly | Basic attribution habits | Legal case studies on training data and AI-output ownership |
| Privacy & personal data | No personal info shared with AI, ever | Understand what a tool logs/stores | Evaluate a tool's data policy before using it in a project |
| Tool access | Instructor-managed only, no individual accounts | School-managed accounts, signed parent agreement before image-gen module | Supervised API accounts, logged usage, signed responsible-use agreement |

**Operating rules for the academy:**

1. No student under 13 is issued an individual account on a consumer generative AI product; all such use is instructor-managed or via a COPPA-compliant, purpose-built school tool with a signed Data Processing Agreement in place.
2. From Tier 2 onward, students and a parent/guardian sign a responsible-use agreement before any image- or video-generation module, explicitly covering the deepfake policy: never create a realistic image, video, or audio of a real person without clear, informed consent — full stop.
3. Deepfake and misinformation content is framed around real, current legal risk (this is a genuinely fast-moving area of law), not just "school rules," because that is the accurate picture and the one that best protects students.
4. Any sample media used for detection practice is either clearly synthetic-and-labeled or drawn from public, non-victim sources — never real nonconsensual content, even for teaching purposes.

## 8. Assessment & Certification

## 9. Instructor & Logistics Notes

- **Cadence:** 2 live sessions/week per cohort (Session A: concept, Session B: studio); tiers can run concurrently across age groups.
- **Class size:** Tier 1: 8–10; Tier 2: 12–16; Tier 3: 16–20 — kept small given the hands-on studio format.
- **Tech requirements:** one device with a browser per student (or pair, in Tier 1); reliable internet for cloud tools; Tier 3 additionally needs a supervised API key and logging in place before Week 8.
- **Compliance:** confirm signed DPAs for any tool touching under-13 students before the course starts; confirm responsible-use agreements are collected before each tier's image/video-generation or API module.
- **Parent communication:** brief note home at the end of each tier summarizing content and the capstone/showcase; explicit, separate communication about the deepfake responsible-use policy before Tier 2, Week 9.
- **Staffing:** Tier 1 benefits from a co-facilitator given the younger age group and instructor-driven tool use; Tiers 2–3 can run with one instructor plus optional TA support during capstone weeks.
